## What stayed with me

Time is not one universal river. It passes differently depending on speed, gravity, and where an observer happens to stand. The familiar shared present is a useful approximation, not a property of the universe.

Rovelli makes this disorientation feel intimate rather than cold. The book moves from physics toward experience without pretending the two are interchangeable.

## Notes in the margin

- Clocks measure change; they do not reveal a separate substance called time.
- Entropy gives time its direction at the scale where human life happens.
- Memory is part of why the past feels fixed and the future open.

## A question to keep

If the present is local rather than universal, how much of daily life depends on treating a practical agreement as an absolute truth?
