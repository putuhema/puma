## A place that can remain unfinished

Most websites are presented as finished things. A notebook makes a different promise: it can hold an observation before the observation becomes an argument.

That changes the way I write. I can publish the useful fragment, return when experience has added something, and let the page keep a visible history of attention.

> The goal is not to publish more. It is to make returning easier.

## The shape of the library

The sections here are deliberately broad. Notes hold fragments. Essays make a longer attempt. Books gather the marks left by reading, while Projects keep the practical work close to the thinking that shaped it.

### Writing without ceremony

Each entry is a plain MDX file. The title, date, and a short summary live at the top; the rest is ordinary Markdown. There is no dashboard waiting between the idea and the page.

### Reading without interruption

The interface stays quiet so the prose can carry the weight. Navigation remains nearby, but nothing competes for the center of the page.

## What belongs here

Anything worth finding again: a durable idea, a useful mistake, a sentence from a book, or the record of a project while its edges are still rough.

The library can become more specific over time. It only needs to begin as a place with enough room.
