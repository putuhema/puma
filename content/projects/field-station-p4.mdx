## The idea

Most portfolios are a header, a grid of cards and a contact link. This one is a television. The whole site is a tape playing on a CRT, with a curved glass face, scanlines, static between channels and a tracking band rolling down the picture. There is no navigation bar. You get around by talking to Mr. P, the station's host, or by tuning channels from the keyboard.

## Mr. P

Mr. P is a low-poly alien in a bubble helmet, drawn in Three.js and rendered as a bitmap so he matches the tube. He drifts around the screen when nobody is talking to him and flies back when you start typing.

His replies stream from a language model through a small route handler that asks for a short line of text, then a control line saying which instrument to mount under the reply and what mood he's in. His face acts out the mood. When the model is unreachable, he answers from a local phrasebook instead, so the site never goes silent.

## Instruments

Replies can carry instruments: the operator's dossier, a tracking scope of notes, this tape shelf, a contact transmitter, and Star Catcher, a small arcade game drawn in ASCII on a canvas. They are ordinary React components mounted inside the speech bubble.

## Live channels

The Sanctuary, the guestbook, the Star Catcher high-score table and the "tuned in" counter run on Convex. Queries are live, so a new message or signature shows up for everyone without polling, and mutations do their own rate limiting.

## Built for the keyboard

Every part of the station can be driven without a mouse: type anywhere to talk, `/` for slash commands with completion, Alt and a number to change channel, `j` and `k` to scroll, and `?` for the full keyboard map. Sound effects are synthesised with the Web Audio API rather than loaded as files, so the clicks and chirps all come from the same machine.
